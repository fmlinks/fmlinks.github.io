Why geometry matters.

Medical images describe anatomy. Meshes make its geometry explicit.

01 / CONTEXT

From patient-specific reconstruction to virtual populations and numerical simulation, anatomical geometry connects imaging with computational medicine. The survey maps the methods, data and evaluation choices behind this transition. A reconstructed surface alone does not establish simulation readiness or clinical benefit.

Four ways to learn anatomy.

02 / METHODS

Template models

Deform or register a reference mesh to the target anatomy. Controlled connectivity and correspondence depend on the chosen template.

The trade-off Stable mesh connectivity; limited flexibility when anatomy differs from the template.

Statistical shape models

Learn anatomical variation through shape priors. Prior constraints can support consistent reconstruction while limiting fine detail.

The trade-off Anatomical regularity; a balance between the learned prior and patient-specific variation.

Generative models

Learn distributions of anatomical shape through latent representations, adversarial learning, interpolation or diffusion.

The trade-off Flexible shape synthesis; training stability, data needs and anatomical validity remain important.

Implicit models

Represent geometry through continuous functions. Detailed surfaces emerge from distance fields, neural dynamics, occupancy or radiance representations.

The trade-off Flexible surface representation; optimisation, topology control and mesh extraction need care.
